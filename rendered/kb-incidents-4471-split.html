The three problems

parser. Every tenant on file writes the day first, and the shared parser reads every date month first. 7 April becomes 4 July. It reaches people through the app, by email and through the booking-v2 form, which asks for DD/MM/YYYY and passes it straight to the parser. The queued fix is for this one.

hint. The booking-v3 form prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field. People type what the hint tells them, month first, and the form reads it the tenant’s way, day first. The result looks the same as the parser bug, a flipped month, but only booking-v3 tenants hit it, only through the form, and fixing the parser will not fix it. The hint and the form have to agree.

one-day-move. Five reports are not a flipped month. The date moves by one day: a Tuesday that shows as Monday, every booking in one month one day off, one meeting that reads Tuesday in Lisbon and Monday in Toronto, an invite that arrived before the booking was made. That is a day boundary crossed, most likely a time zone or UTC conversion, and it is a different bug again. The desk read these as a one-day move when they came in and noted it, but the subject rule filed them under the parser.

How the split was made

  • One id, one report. The export pages overlap, so the same report can appear twice.
  • The problem is the tenant’s: their locale and which booking form they run, from the tenant directory. Intake’s tag is a guess from the reporter’s browser and is wrong often enough that nothing here counts it.
  • A report the desk read as a one-day move belongs to that problem, not to the tenant’s cause.

The table above runs this on every build. If the desk merges more reports into #4471, the counts move with it.

What to do

  1. Keep the parser fix queued, and link it to its ticket.
  2. Change the booking-v3 hint, or read the field the way the hint says. Either one closes the hint ticket.
  3. Open the one-day move as an investigation. Start with R-41344: one meeting, two cities, two days.
  4. Stop merging on subject alone. Decision 0001 was written for speed during an incident, and it is how three problems became one ticket. Merge on cause, with the tenant in hand.
